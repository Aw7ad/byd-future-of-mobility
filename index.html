Dossier / The company

From battery maker to
global mobility group.

Founded in Shenzhen in 1995 as a rechargeable-battery manufacturer, BYD has grown into a multinational group spanning automobiles, electronics, renewable energy and rail transit.

Its advantage is structural: BYD develops and produces batteries, motors, power electronics and semiconductors in-house. That vertical integration shortens development cycles, protects margins and lets the company move quickly from a technology breakthrough to a vehicle on the road.

01Project

Defining the vision

A project turns ambition into a bounded, resourced commitment. Ours: a three-year international expansion programme that would make BYD’s electric mobility locally relevant in new markets.

Project brief

Horizon
2026 — 2029

Priority regions
Europe · Latin America · Southeast Asia

Academic proposal

Strengthen BYD’s international presence through a localized electric-vehicle expansion that pairs technological leadership with sustainable, customer-centred mobility.

This is a proposed project for academic analysis — not a BYD announcement. It gives the five management functions one coherent object to work on, from definition to control.

  • 3 priority regions
  • Localized EV portfolio
  • Partnership-led distribution
  • Customer experience platform
  • Measurable milestones

Project objectives

Five outcomes define success — and later become the basis of control.

01

Increase international presence

Enter selected high-growth markets with a focused product and channel strategy.

02

Strengthen the brand

Build recognition and trust for BYD as a premium electric mobility brand.

03

Promote sustainable transport

Accelerate the shift from combustion to zero-emission mobility in each market.

04

Improve customer experience

Deliver a consistent purchase, charging and service journey across countries.

05

Develop new mobility solutions

Adapt vehicles, charging and digital services to local usage and infrastructure.

Project resources

Four resource families must be assembled and balanced. Select one to explore it.

Human resources

The team that executes the strategy

  • Battery and vehicle engineers
  • Researchers and product designers
  • Marketing and brand teams
  • International sales teams
  • Project management office

Key constraint

Local market knowledge cannot be imported at speed. Each region needs local sales, service and regulatory expertise alongside BYD engineers.

Financial resources

Capital allocated to long-term growth

  • Research & development budget
  • Marketing and brand investment
  • Production and localization investment
  • Distribution and logistics costs
  • Working capital for market launch

Key constraint

Overseas expansion competes with domestic growth for capital. Funding is therefore phased and tied to milestone evidence, not released all at once.

Material resources

Physical capacity to deliver at scale

  • Manufacturing facilities and industrial parks
  • Battery cell and pack production equipment
  • Electric vehicle production lines
  • Logistics and port infrastructure
  • Regional assembly and distribution hubs

Key constraint

Localization reduces tariffs and lead times but demands heavy fixed investment. The project sequences assembly capacity after demand validation.

Technological resources

The differentiation engine

  • Blade Battery technology
  • Electric vehicle platforms (e-Platform)
  • In-house R&D and vertical integration
  • Intelligent vehicle and driver-assistance systems
  • Charging and energy-storage integration

Key constraint

Technology must be adapted, not copied, into each market: homologation, climate performance, charging standards and customer expectations differ.

02Planning

Analyzing the future

Planning connects where BYD is today to where the project needs it to be. A SWOT analysis clarifies the position; a chosen strategy sets the direction; a roadmap turns direction into timed commitments.

S

Strengths

Internal · helpful

  • Deep battery technology expertise
  • Vertical integration across the value chain
  • Competitive electric vehicle portfolio
  • Large research and development capability
  • Strong manufacturing capacity and cost control
W

Weaknesses

Internal · harmful

  • Limited brand recognition in some international markets
  • Dependence on the success of international expansion
  • Difficulty adapting to different market expectations
  • Complex global logistics and after-sales coverage
O

Opportunities

External · helpful

  • Growing global demand for electric vehicles
  • Expansion into emerging markets
  • Government support for sustainable transportation
  • Development of charging infrastructure
  • Strategic international partnerships
T

Threats

External · harmful

  • Strong competition from established manufacturers
  • International trade restrictions and tariffs
  • Changing government regulations
  • Intense price competition
  • Supply chain disruptions

Selected strategy · SO

Use strengths to capture demand.

BYD should leverage its battery expertise, manufacturing capacity, vertical integration and competitive electric-vehicle portfolio to capture growing international demand for sustainable mobility.

The SO strategy is selected because BYD’s rarest asset — control of the full electric powertrain chain — is most valuable where demand is expanding fastest. A WO strategy would require capabilities the company is still building; ST would defend a position already strong; WT would be reactive rather than growth-oriented.

  1. 01Expand into selected international markets with a focused entrance sequence
  2. 02Strengthen local partnerships for distribution, charging and service
  3. 03Increase marketing investment and brand awareness
  4. 04Invest in research and development for local adaptation
  5. 05Improve customer services and distribution networks

Strategy roadmap

Four gated phases. Each phase releases the next tranche of investment.

  1. Phase 01 · 2026

    Market intelligence

    Screen markets, map regulation and tariff exposure, validate demand and select three entry markets.

    Gate: market scorecard approved

  2. Phase 02 · 2027

    Partnerships & homologation

    Sign local distribution and charging partners; adapt vehicles to local standards and climate.

    Gate: compliance and partner readiness

  3. Phase 03 · 2028

    Launch & brand building

    Launch the localized portfolio, build the dealer and service network, run the brand campaign.

    Gate: first-year sales and satisfaction targets

  4. Phase 04 · 2029

    Scale & optimize

    Expand the model range, localize assembly where volumes justify it, optimize cost and service.

    Gate: sustainable unit economics

Illustrative roadmap for the academic project. Dates and gates are proposals, not BYD commitments.

      04Directing

      Leading innovation

      Structure defines where decisions are made; directing defines how people are moved to make them well. The DRIVE method is an original leadership system built for this project.

      Original leadership approach

      The DRIVE method

      Five principles that turn a multinational plan into coordinated daily action. Select a letter to explore it.

      D

      Principle 01 of 05

      Direction

      Define clear objectives and communicate the project’s vision until every team can explain what success looks like, why it matters and how their work contributes to it. Direction replaces ambiguity with a shared destination.

      On the expansion project

      • A three-year vision translated into market-level objectives and team OKRs
      • One page of strategic priorities visible in every regional office
      • Quarterly all-hands to reconnect daily work with the mission
      Use ← → to move between principles

      Leadership in practice

      The method is supported by five concrete management practices.

      Participative & transformational

      Participative leadership brings local teams into decisions they must own; transformational leadership supplies the purpose and ambition that sustain a multi-year expansion. The Director sets direction and standards, then delegates execution.

      Leadership approach

      Communication

      Weekly team meetings, monthly management reviews, and a single shared dashboard so every region works from the same facts.

      Motivation

      Recognition, professional development and visible celebration of team achievements — especially for difficult first entries.

      Decision-making

      Collaborative decisions, supported by performance data rather than hierarchy or opinion. Decisions are logged with an owner and a review date.

      Conflict management

      Open communication and structured problem-solving: separate interests from positions, agree the facts, then choose the option that serves the project.

      05Control

      Measuring performance

      Control closes the loop. Progress is reviewed at three rhythms, compared against targets, and converted into corrective action — not just reported.

      Cadence 01

      Weekly

      Operational

      Are tasks moving?

      • Task completion against the weekly plan
      • Team progress and blockers
      • Operational problems and escalations

      Owner: department leads · 30-minute stand-up

      Cadence 02

      Monthly

      Tactical

      Are resources producing results?

      • Budget monitoring and variance
      • Marketing performance and pipeline
      • Sales progress and production output

      Owner: Project Director · management review

      Cadence 03

      Quarterly

      Strategic

      Is the strategy still right?

      • Customer satisfaction and brand health

      Corrective action

      How managers use the indicators

      A KPI only creates value when it changes a decision. The control loop is deliberately simple so it can run every week.

      1. 01

        Measure

        Collect actuals from sales, finance, production and customer systems on a fixed cadence.

      2. 02

        Compare

        Test actuals against the target and the tolerance band; colour the status green, amber or red.

      3. 03

        Diagnose

        Ask why the variance exists. Separate a data problem, an execution problem and a strategy problem.

      4. 04

        Correct

        Assign an action, an owner and a review date. Escalate only what the level above can actually solve.

      Worked example

      Sales growth sits at 72% of target for two consecutive months. The Sales team diagnoses pipeline conversion, Marketing reallocates spend to the underperforming market, and the Director rebalances the quarterly budget at the next management review.
